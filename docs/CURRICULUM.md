# AeroLab curriculum

## Objective and prerequisites
Turn visual changes into causal aerodynamic reasoning. No prior formula fluency is required. Units are introduced beside controls. The conceptual sequence is airflow → aerofoil → AoA → lift → stall → fixed-lift drag → efficiency → static stability. Modules remain directly accessible for revision; within each module mission tiers unlock sequentially.

| Module / mastery skill | Demonstrated objective | Prerequisite | Learn manipulation | Mission family (four tiers) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Airflow | Predict continuity and pressure changes | None | Halve throat area at fixed inlet speed | The throat gate: achieve a throat speed |
| The aerofoil | Distinguish camber from AoA; interpret CP/AC | Airflow | Produce lift at zero AoA with positive camber | A wing with a purpose: meet CL and thickness targets |
| Angle of attack | Separate attitude from flight path | Geometry | Change path while holding pitch | The deceptive horizon: achieve alpha/path combinations |
| Making lift | Apply rho, V², S and CL relationships | AoA | Double TAS, see fourfold lift | Same lift, thinner air: deliver target lift within limits |
| Beyond the limit | Diagnose critical-angle stall regardless of speed/attitude | CL–alpha | Reduce AoA in a nose-low descending state | Fast, nose low, stalled: restore attached-flow load |
| Drag lab | Interpret induced/parasite drag under fixed lift | Lift and stall | Find near-equal drag contributions | Minimum resistance: tune speed with different mass/AR |
| The efficient wing | Connect L/D with still-air glide distance | Drag | Tune the maximum L/D and compare flap | The distance reserve: regain range and select clean wing |
| Restoring balance | Identify stable, neutral and unstable static tendency | AoA and moment | Place CG ahead of NP | The balance test: produce specified static margin |

## Priority map
**Essential:** first six modules, particularly AoA, lift and stall. **Important:** pressure, camber, configuration, aspect ratio and efficiency. **Advanced:** CP/AC interpretation and whole-aircraft static stability. CP/AC is visible in the geometry experiment but its deeper moment meaning can be revisited after stability.

## Learning loop
Observe the initial state → choose a prediction → reveal controlled before/after values → return to the working setup → manipulate controls → record a measured goal → receive causal feedback → apply the concept in a mission. Predictions are separate controlled experiments, not a blanket interruption on each slider move. A wrong prediction can be repeated; mastery still requires completing the physical manipulation.

## Misconceptions explicitly targeted
- Pitch equals AoA: use independently controlled flight path and pitch, including negative pitch with positive AoA.
- Stall means slow or nose-high: compare stalled states at several speeds; recover in a nose-low descent by changing alpha.
- Lift always equals weight: instantaneous force lab shows acceleration, plus climb/descent component equations.
- Thrust always equals drag: gravity has an along-path component when gamma is nonzero.
- Doubling speed doubles lift: controlled prediction demonstrates V² with fixed CL.
- Any speed increase lowers induced drag: only in the fixed-lift drag lab; ordinary wing mode keeps the chosen AoA.
- Flaps always improve glide: compare drag at the same required lift and speed.
- Wing CP alone decides aircraft stability: separate CP/AC from whole-aircraft NP.
- Air parcels must reunite at the trailing edge: no paired parcel animation or equal-transit-time explanation is used.
- Bernoulli is a complete standalone theory of lift: it is applied to an ideal tube; wing forces require surface pressure/shear, with the force model separately specified.

## Mastery and progression
Each skill has 0–100 mastery. A completed Learn experiment contributes 30 only after a correct prediction checkpoint and a deliberate control adjustment meeting its target. Best mission scores contribute 10/15/20/25 weighted points across Training/Standard/Advanced/Captain. Opening pages and Lab snapshots award nothing. Repeating a passed run does not accumulate points; a higher best score can improve mastery. Lesson and mission progress are independent, allowing experienced students to begin with Training missions.

All eight modules are available. Standard is unlocked by a passed Training mission in that module, Advanced by Standard, Captain by Advanced. Tiers increase precision or add constraints; they do not add fictitious physical complexity. Stability Captain intentionally requests an unstable state as a diagnostic experiment, and is not penalized for meeting that goal.

## Scope
English interface and educational explanations. No certified training credit. High-lift devices are synthetic presets, planform scope is rectangular span/chord/AR, pressure is qualitative, and stability is static only. Detailed sweep, taper, dynamic modes, full flight trajectories, pilot control techniques, CFD and compressibility are deferred.
